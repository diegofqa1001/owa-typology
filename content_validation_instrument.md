# Content-validation instrument — confirmatory expert phase

Instrument accompanying "From Behavioral Finance to Explainable Investment Recommendation:
A Fuzzy-OWA Typology of Investor Risk Profiles". To be administered to a panel of 10–12
experts (behavioral finance / financial psychology; fuzzy decision making / recommender systems).

## Response format
Five-point Likert scale: 1 = strongly disagree … 5 = strongly agree.
Panelists rate every item and may add free-text comments per section.

## Decision rules (fixed in advance)
- Median ≥ 4 and CVR ≥ 0.78 → validated with minor adjustments
- Median ≥ 4 and CVR < 0.78 → second round with targeted feedback
- Median < 4 → major revision required

CVR = (n_e − N/2)/(N/2), with n_e the number of panelists rating the item 4 or 5
(Lawshe, 1975; critical values per Wilson et al., 2012).

## Section A — Behavioral dimensions (7 dimensions × 4 criteria = 28 items)
For each of D1 Risk tolerance, D2 Loss aversion, D3 Financial self-efficacy,
D4 Ambiguity tolerance, D5 Investment horizon, D6 Emotional regulation,
D7 Perceived social influence:
- A1 Clarity: the definition of this dimension is clear and unambiguous.
- A2 Relevance: this dimension is relevant for characterizing investor risk behavior.
- A3 Independence: this dimension captures variance not covered by the others.
- A4 Operationalizability: this dimension can be measured with existing validated instruments.

## Section B — Prototypical profiles (8 profiles × 4 criteria = 32 items)
For each of P1 Guardian … P8 Visionary:
- B1 Internal coherence: the combination of linguistic values is psychologically plausible.
- B2 Differentiation: this profile is distinguishable from the remaining profiles.
- B3 Representativeness: this profile corresponds to a recognizable type of real investor.
- B4 Utility: assigning an investor to this profile would be useful for advisory purposes.

PRIORITY (per the robustness analysis, Section 4.2 of the paper): items B2 for
P4 Adventurer, P5 Strategist and P6 Analyst — the least-resolved region of the typology.

## Section C — Global system (5 items)
- C1 The seven dimensions jointly cover the relevant space of investor risk behavior.
- C2 The eight profiles jointly cover the relevant space of investor types.
- C3 The typology is superior to the conventional conservative/moderate/aggressive triad.
- C4 The fuzzy linguistic formalization is adequate for representing these constructs.
- C5 Deriving the aggregation attitude (orness) from the profile vector is coherent.

## Panel composition
Subpanel A (content, n ≈ 5–6): behavioral finance, financial psychology, investor profiling.
Subpanel B (method, n ≈ 5–6): fuzzy sets and aggregation operators, MCDM, recommender systems.
Inclusion criteria: doctorate or equivalent professional standing, ≥ 5 years of relevant
experience, peer-reviewed publications or professional practice in the area.

## Ethics
Expert judgement study on a methodological artifact; participants are professionals rating a
framework, not human subjects providing personal data. Informed consent covering purpose,
voluntary participation, anonymity of individual responses and aggregate-only reporting is
obtained before participation.
